The main shared-custody models

In Québec, "shared" custody means each parent assumes at least 40% of custody time. No model is imposed by law: the goal is to choose a formula that serves the child's best interest and works for both households. The most common models are:

  • 7/7 (week on, week off) — the child alternates one full week at each parent's home, typically with a Friday-after-school transition. Easy to remember, ideal for children 7 and up.
  • 5/5/2/2 — 5 days with parent A, 5 days with parent B, then 2/2. Keeps the same weekdays at each parent's home (e.g., Mom always Mon-Fri, Dad always weekends + 3 days).
  • 3-2-2 or 2-2-3 — shorter alternation, especially suited to children under 6 who tolerate long separations poorly.
  • Alternating weekends + one weeknight — known as "primary custody". One parent handles most of the daily routine, the other sees the child one weeknight and every other weekend.
  • Custom schedule — tailored to fit shift work, FIFO rotations, custody alternation with half-siblings, etc. Our tool lets you build this kind of calendar by clicking directly on the days.
📌 Good to know: this calculator imposes no model. You can freely combine approaches (e.g., 2-2-3 during the school year, 7/7 during vacations) and export everything to a single file.

How the tool calculates your calendar

  1. Choose the start date — usually when the new custody schedule takes effect (separation, court order, school year start, etc.).
  2. Select the display duration: 1 month to see the rhythm, 6 months or 1 year to share with the school or grandparents, up to 2 years for long-term judgments.
  3. Choose the custody type and configure the names (Mom, Dad, or any first names) and the color associated with each home.
  4. Read the generated calendar, month by month. Each day shows where the child is, and the stats bar summarizes the time split.
  5. Export to PDF (to print or attach to email), .ics (for Apple Calendar, Outlook, Google Calendar) or use direct print.

Custody and child support calculation

In Québec, the Québec model for the determination of child support takes into account each parent's custody time. The exact percentage of days/nights at each home directly influences the support calculation:

  • Exclusive custody — one parent has 60% or more of the time. Support is calculated using the standard grid from the Québec Ministry of Justice.
  • Shared custody — each parent has between 40% and 60% of the time. The calculation is different and accounts for each parent's disposable income, special expenses (daycare, activities, medical), and the exact proportion of custody time.
  • Primary custody with access — one parent has less than 40% of the time. Support calculated as in exclusive custody, but with a possible credit for direct expenses borne by the access parent.

Our calculator doesn't compute the support itself, but the exact percentage of custody time it displays is valuable input for the government tools or your family lawyer.

Tips for a calendar that lasts

  1. Anticipate holidays and school breaks — explicitly alternate Christmas, Easter, National Holiday and spring break. Our tool lets you add exceptions to your base calendar.
  2. Plan transitions — specify the time and location of the handover (school rather than the other parent's home, for example). This significantly reduces friction.
  3. Put everything in writing — even if you agree verbally, an exported calendar accepted by both parents serves as evidence in case of future disagreement.
  4. Share the calendar with trusted third parties — school, daycare, doctor, grandparents. The .ics format is universal and auto-updates for all recipients if you republish it.
  5. Reassess each year — children's needs change with age. A 2-2-3 model ideal at 4 may become constraining at 12, and vice versa.

Frequently asked questions

Must shared custody be strictly 50/50?

No. Legal shared custody in Québec is defined starting at 40% of time for each parent. Many families operate with a 45/55 or 47/53 split depending on the formula chosen, and this still counts as shared custody for child support and family tax credits.

At what age can a child choose their custody arrangement?

In Québec, there's no fixed legal age. However, courts generally give increasing weight to the child's opinion from age 12, and decisive weight from age 14. Before that age, the deciding criterion remains the child's best interest as assessed by a judge, often with the help of an expert (psychologist, social worker).

How do we handle holidays and birthdays?

The most common method is yearly alternation: Christmas with Mom in even years, with Dad in odd years, for example. For the child's birthday, many families share the day (one parent in the morning, the other in the evening) or organize two separate parties. Our tool lets you mark these exceptions without changing the base rhythm.

Is my custody data confidential?

Absolutely. All calculations happen in your browser — no data (names, dates, custody model) is sent to a CalculDates.ca server. The PDF or .ics file is generated locally and downloaded directly to your device.

Can this tool replace a written agreement or court order?

No. The generated calendar is a visualization and communication tool, not a legal document. For a calendar that has enforceable status in a dispute, you must have it court-approved or incorporated into a notarized agreement. You can however use the generated PDF as an attachment to your agreement.
